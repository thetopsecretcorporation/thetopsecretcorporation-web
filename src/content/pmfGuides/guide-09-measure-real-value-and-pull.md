## Field Brief
You do not have product-market fit because users signed up.

**Product-market fit** means a specific customer keeps getting value, keeps using or paying, and starts pulling more of the product from you.

**Pull** means customers do extra work to keep, expand, share, or buy the product.

## Why It Matters
Signups are easy to inflate. Repeat value is harder to fake.

Customers show pull when they come back, pay, renew, add teammates, tolerate rough edges, refer another team, or ask for more without being pushed.

## What to Do
### 1. Define the value event
A **value event** is the action that proves the customer got the promised result.

Better than "logged in":
- Hiring manager sends a candidate shortlist to the interview panel.
- Finance marks a found overcharge as recoverable.
- Ops manager resolves a late-order risk before a customer complains.

### 2. Measure behavior around that event
Track:
- Activation into first value.
- Time to first value.
- Repeat value at the natural cadence.
- Retention by customer segment.
- Paid conversion, renewal, or expansion.
- Team adoption.

A monthly workflow should not be judged like a daily chat app. Match retention to the natural frequency of the job.

### 3. Watch for pull
Strong pull examples:
- Customer asks to add three teammates.
- Champion introduces another department without being asked.
- Buyer asks for annual pricing.
- User keeps using it despite rough edges.
- Customer brings the problem to you before the next sales push.

### 4. Use the Sean Ellis survey carefully
The Sean Ellis survey asks active users:

> How would you feel if you could no longer use this product?

The common benchmark is about 40% answering "very disappointed." Treat it as a useful signal, not a law. Survey only users who reached real value recently.

Superhuman's well-known First Round writeup shows how Rahul Vohra used this survey, segmented by strongest users, and improved the score over time.

### 5. Build the scorecard
Use five signals:
- Value event reached.
- Repeat value / retention.
- Money or budget commitment.
- Pull: expansion, referral, urgency.
- Survey dependence.

Then decide: continue, narrow, fix, or stop.

## What Counts
False signal: "1,000 launch signups."  
Stronger signal: "42 right-fit users reached first value, 29 repeated the value event the next week, 7 invited teammates, and 4 asked about annual plans."
