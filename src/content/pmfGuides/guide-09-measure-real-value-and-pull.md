## Brief
PMF is not proven by one metric. It is a pattern across what customers say, what they do, and what they commit.

Marc Andreessen defines product-market fit as "being in a good market with a product that can satisfy that market." He also describes the opposite clearly: "The customers aren't quite getting value out of the product, word of mouth isn't spreading, usage isn't growing that fast..."

This lesson turns those signs into a visible operating artifact: a **PMF Scorecard**. The scorecard should keep the team honest about whether customers are receiving real value, returning at the expected cadence, paying or committing, and pulling the product into their workflow.

Before measuring, make the operating definitions explicit. A **value event** is observable behavior showing the customer received the promised outcome. **Natural frequency** is how often that value event should reasonably happen. **Cohort retention** shows whether customers who started in the same period keep performing the value event over time. Use Net Promoter Score (NPS) only as a supporting sentiment signal, never as proof by itself.

Do not let signups, demos, press, compliments, survey scores, or revenue alone declare PMF. They may be useful signals, but only retained value and customer pull show whether the product is becoming necessary.

## Exercise
Brian Balfour puts the bias in the right place: "I would much rather take bets on what people are doing, not what they say they would do or are doing." Use that standard while you build the scorecard.

### 01. Define the value event.
Capture the promised outcome, the first value moment, the repeat value behavior, and the natural frequency. A business-to-business invoicing product, for example, might define retained value as "the company sends at least 10 invoices per month through the product for three consecutive months," not "someone logged in."

### 02. Pull behavioral evidence.
Measure activation into first value, time to first value, repeat value events, cohort retention, workflow depth, team/account adoption, paid conversion, renewal, and expansion. Retention only means something when it matches the product's natural frequency.

### 03. Run the Sean Ellis survey carefully.
Ask qualified recent users: "How would you feel if you could no longer use this product?" The common benchmark is roughly 40% answering "very disappointed," but treat it as a directional operating signal, not a universal law. Survey only users who reached core value, used the product more than once, and used it recently.

### 04. Ask the follow-ups.
Capture who would benefit most, the main benefit received, how the product could improve, and what the customer would use if the product disappeared. The follow-ups often matter more than the percentage.

### 05. Segment for highest pull.
Break results out by ICP fit, role, company type, use case, acquisition source, activation path, commitment level, retained behavior, and customer language. The Superhuman PMF Engine is useful because it uses the "very disappointed" group to find pockets where the product resonates most strongly.

### 06. Separate pull from false positives.
Strong pull looks like repeat usage, referrals, expansion, customer-initiated introductions, inbound requests, faster sales cycles, budget commitment, and tolerance of rough edges. Weak signals include launch spikes, demo enthusiasm, generic compliments, free users with no commitment, NPS without retention, and one bespoke customer pulling the roadmap away from the ICP.

### 07. Make a stage decision.
Choose one: continue, narrow, iterate, or do not scale. If retention trends toward zero, Balfour's warning applies: "Without retention, accelerating growth is meaningless. Your retention curve is the best proof."

## Deliverable
Produce a **PMF Scorecard** that the whole founding team and key employees can see. Put it in the company wiki, operating dashboard, strategy doc, or product hub. It should be reviewed weekly until the stage decision changes.

Rahul Vohra's Superhuman case shows why the deliverable must be segment-specific: the goal is not an average score; it is to find the customer group where dependence, behavior, and pull reinforce each other.

Your PMF Scorecard must include:

- **Value definition:** promised outcome, value event, first value moment, repeat behavior, and natural frequency.
- **Behavioral evidence:** activation, time to value, repeat value-event rate, cohort retention, workflow depth, team/account adoption, and paid conversion/renewal/expansion.
- **Survey evidence:** qualified response count, percent "very disappointed," sample caveats, and the main alternative if the product disappeared.
- **Segment insight:** highest-pull customer group, what they share, and how they differ from weaker segments.

- **Customer language:** exact quotes about the main benefit, urgency, disappointment, and current alternative.
- **Commitment evidence:** payment, renewal, expansion, referrals, introductions, inbound demand, or internal champion behavior.
- **False positives:** the signals the team will not overvalue this cycle.
- **Stage decision:** continue, narrow, iterate, or do not scale, with one paragraph of rationale.

A simple decision rule keeps the artifact useful:
- **Continue** when the best-fit segment shows dependence, retained behavior, and early pull.
- **Narrow** when one segment is clearly stronger than the rest.
- **Iterate** when signal exists but the persona, problem, promise, product, channel, or business model needs a focused change.
- **Do not scale** when usage is shallow, retention decays, the survey sample is weak, and customers are not paying, renewing, referring, or pulling the product into workflow.

## Exit Criteria
Move forward only when this checklist is complete. First Round's PMF levels are useful here because they treat PMF as staged, not binary.

- Value event, natural frequency, cohort retention, and NPS are defined before use.
- The core value event is observable and tied to the promised customer outcome.
- Retention is measured against the product's natural usage frequency.
- Sean Ellis survey results are based on qualified, recent users and clearly caveated.

- Results are segmented by customer type, use case, source, commitment, and retained behavior.
- Highest-pull customer language is captured in the customer's own words.
- False positives are explicitly listed.
- The final decision is continue, narrow, iterate, or do not scale.

- The PMF Scorecard is visible to the founding team and key employees.
