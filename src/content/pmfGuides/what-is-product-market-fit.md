Product-market fit (PMF) means a specific market has an urgent problem, your product solves it well enough to become part of how customers work or live, and demand starts pulling the product forward.

Marc Andreessen's classic definition is "being in a good market with a product that can satisfy that market." Michael Seibel's operator version is sharper: you know you are close when you are "overwhelmed with usage."

The practical definition is behavioral. PMF is not what people say in a pitch meeting. It is what they repeatedly do: come back, keep using, pay, renew, expand, refer peers, tolerate rough edges, and create pressure on support, onboarding, sales, roadmap, or capacity.

The short version: **product-market fit is when the market starts pulling the product out of your hands.**

That is why false signals are dangerous. Signups, waitlists, press, investor interest, launch spikes, or one-off custom clients can look like momentum before customers prove retention, payment, repeatability, and pull. A few loud buyers asking for different custom products is not a market. It is noise with revenue attached.

The rest of this field guide turns PMF into a sequence of evidence: who the customer is, what painful job they need solved, whether they will commit, whether the product creates retained value, and whether the company should scale only after the pull is real.
