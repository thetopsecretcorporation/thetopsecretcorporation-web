## Field Brief
"Sounds interesting" is not evidence.

**Willingness to pay** means proof the customer will trade something valuable for the result: money, time, data, access, authority, reputation, or a real next step.

A real customer moves the deal forward.

## Why It Matters
Founders drown in fake yeses:
- "Cool idea."
- "Send me the beta."
- "Keep me posted."
- "We would pay once you build X."

None of that counts unless the customer gives up something scarce.

## What to Do
### 1. Define the commitment you need
Before the call, write:

> We believe [ICP] will commit [money/time/access/authority/reputation] to solve [problem] because [business outcome].

### 2. Know the signal levels
**Weak:** praise, newsletter signup, "send me the beta," vague second call.

**Medium:** data sample, intro to another stakeholder, detailed workflow review, scheduled technical call.

**Strong:** paid pilot, procurement intro, signed pilot scope, production access, internal champion, deadline, success metric.

Concrete strong signal:

> The VP Ops introduces procurement, shares six months of order data, and agrees to a $2,500 paid pilot ending May 31.

### 3. Use the right offer
A **design partner** is an early customer who gives real access and feedback in exchange for helping shape the solution.

An **LOI** is a written intent to buy if specific conditions are met. It is only useful if it names the buyer, scope, timing, and next step.

A free beta is not willingness-to-pay evidence by itself. It can teach product usage, but it does not prove buying intent.

### 4. Ask direct commitment questions
- "If this worked, who would approve it?"
- "What budget would this come from?"
- "What would make this worth paying for?"
- "Would you commit to a paid pilot with [scope] by [date]?"
- "Can we use next week's real workflow/data?"
- "Who else needs to be in the room?"

### 5. Log what changed after the call
If nothing changed, the evidence is weak.

Track:
- Prospect.
- Commitment requested.
- Commitment received.
- Cost to customer.
- Buyer path.
- Objection.
- Next step with date.

## What Counts
Strong commitment looks like money, access, authority, dates, and a buyer path. Enthusiasm without movement is noise.
