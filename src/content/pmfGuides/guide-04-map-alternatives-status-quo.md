## Field Brief
Your biggest competitor is usually not another startup. It is the ugly workaround the customer already trusts.

The **status quo** is the current way the customer survives the problem. An **alternative** is anything they use instead of you: software, a spreadsheet, an intern, a vendor, a manager, a meeting, or doing nothing.

A **switching cost** is anything that makes changing feel expensive, risky, annoying, or politically dangerous.

## Why It Matters
Customers do not compare you to your pitch. They compare you to what they already do.

A RevOps lead who exports Salesforce data into Google Sheets, fixes account owners manually, and sends screenshots to sales managers is not waiting for "a better platform." She has a process. It may be ugly, but it works well enough to survive.

## What to Do
### 1. List the real alternatives
Only count alternatives customers actually use, pay for, tried, or seriously evaluated.

Use five buckets:
- Existing product.
- Spreadsheet or manual workaround.
- Internal person or team.
- Agency, consultant, or vendor.
- Doing nothing or delaying.

Examples:
- A clinic manager handles denied claims by asking one billing specialist to review PDFs every Friday.
- A customer success team ignores churn-risk accounts until renewal month because no one owns account cleanup.
- A finance lead copies Stripe, Shopify, and bank exports into one sheet before close.

### 2. Find why the current way survives
Do not only ask what is broken. Ask what is good enough.

Capture:
- What they like about it.
- What it costs.
- Who owns it.
- What breaks.
- Why they have not changed already.
- What proof would make switching safe.

### 3. Avoid fake switching questions
Do not ask, "Would you switch?" or "Would you pay?"

Ask:
- "How do you handle this today?"
- "What happened the last time this broke?"
- "What else have you tried?"
- "Why did you keep the current process?"
- "Who would need to approve a change?"
- "What would make trying something new feel safe?"

### 4. Write the switching-risk statement
Use this:

> Customers keep [current alternative] because [strengths/habits/risks]. To switch, they need proof that [new solution] preserves [must-have] while improving [painful cost].

## What Counts
You are done when you can name the real alternative, explain why it survives, and define the proof required to beat it.
