## Field Brief
Turn founder-led selling into a simple, repeatable operating loop. Document who you sell to, why they care now, how you reach them, how you qualify them, how you move them through a buying process, and what evidence shows the motion can work again with similar customers.

Repeatable does not mean perfectly scalable. It means the founder can identify likely buyers, reach them through a known path, run a consistent conversation, qualify them with the same criteria, and produce enough similar outcomes to improve the process.

## Why It Matters
Before scaling, the founder must prove that customers can be found, converted, activated, and learned from repeatedly. Early sales is not just revenue work. It is product discovery with money attached.

A repeatable sales motion shows that demand is not random, founder magic is becoming a process, and the company knows how to turn a target customer into a paying customer who reaches value. It also protects the company from hiring sales too early.

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this guide, founders should be able to:

| Skill | What good looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Sales motion hypothesis | The motion fits the product, buyer, ACV, trust requirements, and buying process. |
| ICP and target list | Prospects are selected by pain, trigger, urgency, access, and disqualification rules. |
| Outreach and discovery | The founder can repeat outreach, qualification, follow-up, and learning loops. |
| Pipeline discipline | CRM stages separate real opportunities from interest, compliments, and vague next steps. |
| Guide deliverable | A founder-led sales playbook is clear enough that another person could attempt to run it. |

## Recommended Exercise
### 01. Choose the motion hypothesis
Pick one primary early motion and explain why it fits the product and buyer.

| Motion option | When it may fit |
| --- | --- |
| Self-serve | The buyer can understand, try, and adopt the product without heavy trust-building. |
| Founder-led outbound | The ICP is narrow enough to identify and contact directly. |
| Founder-led inbound | Demand already exists through content, community, referrals, or search. |
| Sales-led B2B | The purchase requires trust, budget ownership, stakeholder alignment, or evaluation. |
| Product-led growth | Product usage can create expansion, sharing, or conversion signal. |
| Community/content-led | Buyers need education, credibility, and repeated exposure before purchase. |
| Channel/partner-led | Access, trust, or distribution depends on an existing intermediary. |

Then document the riskiest assumption in the motion:

| Risk area | Question to answer |
| --- | --- |
| Prospecting | Can you find enough right-fit prospects? |
| Outreach | Can you get replies from the right people? |
| Urgency | Can discovery turn interest into a now problem? |
| Value | Can you prove ROI or business impact? |
| Buying process | Can you navigate procurement, security, budget, or legal? |
| Activation | Can customers onboard successfully and retain after first value? |

### 02. Define an operational ICP
Move beyond broad firmographics. A useful early ICP explains who is likely to buy, why the problem matters, and why now.

| ICP layer | What to document |
| --- | --- |
| Company criteria | Industry, size, stage, geography, tech stack, business model, compliance context, or operating model. |
| Persona criteria | User, buyer, champion, economic buyer, technical evaluator, procurement/legal/security stakeholder if relevant. |
| Pain criteria | What must be broken, expensive, risky, slow, or frustrating enough for the buyer to care. |
| Trigger criteria | Hiring, funding, regulation, missed target, new initiative, renewal, migration, leadership change, budget cycle, product launch, outage, or workflow volume threshold. |
| Current alternative | Spreadsheet, internal tool, agency, incumbent vendor, manual process, status quo, or doing nothing. |
| Disqualification criteria | No urgent pain, wrong segment, no buyer access, no path to budget, feature gap too large, implementation burden too high, or current alternative is good enough. |

### 03. Build the first target list
Create 50-100 real prospects that match the ICP. Mark the top 20 based on urgency and access, not brand prestige.

| Field | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Company/account | Identifies the prospect clearly. |
| Buyer and user names | Shows whether you can reach the right people. |
| Contact path and source | Records how you found them and how you can reach them. |
| ICP reason | Explains why this prospect belongs on the list. |
| Trigger or “why now” evidence | Captures the urgency signal. |
| Likely current alternative | Frames the status quo you are replacing. |
| Priority score | Ranks urgency and access. |
| Disqualification risk | Names the most likely reason the account may not fit. |

### 04. Write and test outreach
Write one primary email or DM under 150 words. It should contain six pieces of information:

| Outreach element | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Who you help | Makes the target clear. |
| Acute problem | Shows relevance quickly. |
| Current alternative or status quo | Proves you understand how the work happens today. |
| Specific outcome | Makes the value concrete. |
| Credibility or proof | Gives the buyer a reason to believe you, if available. |
| Simple ask | Creates a clear call or next step. |

Also create three useful variants:

1. A referral version for executives or contacts who may not own the problem: “Who owns this?”
2. 2-3 follow-ups that add new information instead of repeating “checking in.”
3. A version for each priority segment if the pain or trigger differs.

Track reply rate by segment, message, source, and trigger. Do not conclude that “sales does not work” after a tiny sample. Work backward from the number of customers you need and calculate the outreach volume required.

### 05. Run discovery as qualification, not pitching
Use discovery to understand the buyer’s world before demonstrating the product.

| Discovery area | Core question |
| --- | --- |
| Urgency | “Why solve this now?” |
| Consequence | “What happens if you do nothing?” |
| Current alternative | “How do you solve this today?” |
| Cost of pain | “What does the current process cost in time, money, risk, lost revenue, or frustration?” |
| Stakeholders | “Who else is involved?” |
| Buying history | “How have you bought tools like this before?” |
| Budget | “What budget would this come from?” |
| Success criteria | “What outcome would make this a no-brainer?” |
| Deal risk | “What would block the deal?” |

End every call with one of two outcomes:

| Outcome | How to record it |
| --- | --- |
| Dated next step | Owner, date, and purpose are written down. |
| Clean disqualification | Reason is recorded and the opportunity is no longer treated as active. |

A discovery call without a dated next step should not be treated as an active opportunity.

### 06. Use a qualification rubric
For most early founder-led B2B sales, use CHAMP-lite by default.

| CHAMP-lite criterion | Qualification question |
| --- | --- |
| Challenge | Is the pain specific, repeated, and costly? |
| Authority | Are you speaking with the buyer or a credible champion with access to the buyer? |
| Money | Is there existing budget, a plausible budget owner, or enough business impact to create budget? |
| Prioritization | Is this a now problem, not a someday problem? |
| Next step | Is the next meeting or action scheduled with a date, owner, and purpose? |

For higher-ACV or multi-stakeholder deals, add MEDDIC-lite.

| MEDDIC-lite addition | Qualification question |
| --- | --- |
| Metrics | What measurable outcome matters? |
| Economic buyer | Who owns the money? |
| Decision process | What steps must happen before purchase? |
| Pain | What business pain is being solved? |
| Champion | Who will advocate internally, and what have they done to prove it? |

Separate interest from qualified opportunity. Curiosity, compliments, and “keep me posted” are not pipeline unless the qualification standard is met.

### 07. Define CRM stages and exit criteria
A spreadsheet is fine at this stage. The discipline matters more than the software.

Every prospect record should include source, ICP reason, trigger event, contact path, stage, last touch, next step, qualification status, objections, close outcome, and notes/learnings.

Use stages with clear exit criteria:

| Stage | Exit criteria |
| --- | --- |
| Prospect | ICP fit and valid contact confirmed. |
| Contacted | Outreach sent. |
| Replied | Prospect engaged or referred you to a relevant owner. |
| Discovery scheduled/completed | Conversation happened or is on the calendar. |
| Qualified | Qualification rubric met and next step scheduled. |
| Pilot/proposal/evaluation | Buyer agreed to evaluate against explicit success criteria. |
| Commercial/legal/procurement | Pricing, purchase path, legal, security, or procurement is active. |
| Closed won | Buyer committed and commercial terms are accepted. |
| Closed lost/no decision | Outcome recorded with reason. |
| Onboarded/activated | Customer reached the first value milestone. |

### 08. Track funnel math weekly
Choose a target number of closed customers. Estimate conversion rates backward, then replace assumptions with actual data after the first sprint.

| Funnel metric | What it diagnoses |
| --- | --- |
| Prospects added/contacted | Whether enough right-fit accounts are entering the motion. |
| Reply rate and meeting booked rate | Whether the ICP, trigger, channel, and message are working. |
| Discovery-to-qualified rate | Whether pain, persona, urgency, and qualification standards are strong enough. |
| Qualified-to-pilot/proposal rate | Whether the demo, proof, pricing, and urgency are compelling. |
| Proposal/pilot-to-close and win rate | Whether budget, trust, decision process, onboarding burden, or ROI is resolved. |
| Average sales cycle and ACV/pilot value | Whether the motion fits the business model. |
| Next-step scheduled rate | Whether conversations are moving or drifting. |
| Top objections and lost reasons | What must change in ICP, product, proof, pricing, or messaging. |
| Time to first value after close | Whether closed-won customers actually activate. |

Use the math to diagnose the motion:

| Funnel symptom | Likely diagnosis |
| --- | --- |
| Low reply rate | The ICP, trigger, channel, or message may be wrong. |
| Low discovery-to-qualified rate | The pain may be weak or the wrong persona may be responding. |
| Low qualified-to-proposal rate | The demo, proof, pricing, or urgency may be weak. |
| Low close rate | Budget, trust, decision process, onboarding burden, or ROI may be unresolved. |
| Low activation after close | The sale may have overpromised or onboarding may be too hard. |

### 09. Build the first sales asset kit
Create enough material to make the motion repeatable without turning it into a polished enterprise sales machine.

| Asset | Job |
| --- | --- |
| One-page positioning/pitch | Gives buyers a concise explanation of the problem, audience, outcome, and proof. |
| Discovery call guide | Keeps conversations consistent and focused on qualification. |
| Demo script tied to pain | Shows the product through buyer outcomes, not features. |
| Pricing or pilot offer | Makes the next commercial step concrete. |
| Objection-response document | Turns repeated friction into prepared responses and product/positioning decisions. |
| Security/trust/FAQ doc | Handles trust concerns when relevant. |
| Buyer-forwardable summary | Packages problem, outcome, proof, price, implementation path, and next step for internal sharing. |

### 10. Turn objections into actions
Record repeated objections and decide what each one means.

| Common objection | Possible response category |
| --- | --- |
| No budget | Pricing or packaging issue; qualification filter. |
| No urgency | Messaging fix; qualification filter; true disqualification rule. |
| Current process is good enough | Proof asset; positioning fix; true disqualification rule. |
| Missing feature | Product gap or one-off request to ignore. |
| Switching risk | Proof asset; onboarding change. |
| Trust/security concern | Security/trust/FAQ asset. |
| Unclear ROI | Proof asset; messaging fix. |
| Too hard to implement | Onboarding change; product gap. |
| Wrong stakeholder | Qualification filter; buyer-forwardable summary. |

Objections are not just blockers. They are data about ICP, positioning, product, trust, pricing, and timing.

### 11. Include onboarding and activation
The sales motion does not end at “closed won.” A customer who buys but never reaches value is weak PMF evidence.

| Activation field | What to define |
| --- | --- |
| First value milestone | The first concrete outcome that proves the customer reached value. |
| Onboarding owner | The person accountable for getting the customer activated. |
| Activation steps | The sequence required to reach first value. |
| Required customer actions | What the customer must do for onboarding to work. |
| Expected time to first value | The realistic timeline from close to first value. |
| Early success signal | The first measurable sign that the customer is succeeding. |
| Expansion or reference potential | Whether the account may grow, refer, or become proof. |

Track where onboarding fails. If customers need custom work, hand-holding, or long implementation before seeing value, that belongs in the sales motion and the product roadmap.

### 12. Review the first 10-20 sales conversations
After the first sprint, review patterns across four lenses.

| Lens | Questions to answer |
| --- | --- |
| Segment and trigger | Which segment replied fastest? Which pain created urgency? Which trigger predicted a real opportunity? |
| Persona and message | Which persona had authority or champion potential? Which message got replies? |
| Objections and stalls | Which objections repeated? Which deals stalled and why? |
| Product and activation | Which product gaps were real blockers versus one-off requests? Which customers reached first value fastest? |

Then decide what must change in ICP, message, demo, price, product, onboarding, or qualification. Continue only if the motion is repeatable enough; otherwise, run another wedge iteration.

## Sales Hiring Gate
Do not hire sales to compensate for founder uncertainty. A first sales hire should execute, stress-test, and improve a motion that already has evidence. They should not be expected to invent the ICP, message, offer, and buying process from scratch.

### Do not hire yet if
| Warning sign | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| The founder cannot sell the product at all | There is no proven motion to hand off. |
| Wins are only from friends, investors, or warm favors | Demand may not exist outside the network. |
| The ICP is broad or unstable | A salesperson will not know who to target. |
| There is no consistent outreach message | The company has not found a repeatable wedge. |
| Discovery does not identify repeatable pain | Calls are not producing a clear buying reason. |
| Pipeline stages are undefined | Activity cannot be separated from real progress. |
| Objections and close reasons are unclear | The company cannot learn from wins and losses. |
| Customers do not activate after purchase | Revenue is not yet strong PMF evidence. |
| The company cannot explain why a salesperson would win the next deal | Hiring would outsource unresolved founder learning. |

### Consider hiring only when
| Readiness signal | Evidence needed |
| --- | --- |
| Non-friendly prospects are advancing or closing | The pattern is not just personal network leverage. |
| Segment, trigger, message, and qualification are documented | The motion can be attempted by someone else. |
| The same process produces replies and qualified opportunities | Demand is repeatable enough to test. |
| Won and lost reasons cluster into understandable patterns | The team knows why deals move or fail. |
| Onboarding reaches a repeatable first value milestone | Customers can activate after purchase. |
| The playbook is clear | A first seller can execute, stress-test, and improve the motion. |

## Guide Deliverable
Create a founder-led sales playbook that answers six operating questions.

| Operating question | Deliverable sections |
| --- | --- |
| What motion are we testing? | Motion hypothesis and product-market-sales fit rationale. |
| Who are we selling to? | Operational ICP, disqualification rules, target list rules, and first 50-100 prospects. |
| How do we create and qualify demand? | Outreach sequence, message variants, discovery guide, and CHAMP-lite or MEDDIC-lite rubric. |
| How do we manage the process? | CRM stages, exit criteria, and funnel metrics dashboard. |
| What are we learning? | Objection library with action decisions and 10-20 conversation review. |
| Can someone else attempt the motion? | Sales asset kit, onboarding/activation plan, repeatability evidence, and sales hiring gate decision. |

The deliverable should show whether the same narrow ICP, message, channel, qualification standard, sales process, and onboarding path can repeatedly turn right-fit prospects into activated customers.
