## Brief
Founder-led sales is a learning system before it is a hiring system. It proves whether the same type of customer can be found, reached, qualified, converted, onboarded, and activated repeatedly.

Founding Sales defines the work as the process by which founders "discover, refine, and scale their product's initial sales motion." That includes "messaging, ideal customer profile, prospecting, sales materials, and more needed to close the first few dozen customers themselves."

Use the course definitions for PMF, ICP, MVP, and ACV. The sales-specific tools here are **CHAMP**—Challenges, Authority, Money, Prioritization—and **MEDDIC**—Metrics, Economic buyer, Decision criteria/process, Identify pain, Champion. Use CHAMP for lightweight qualification and MEDDIC when deal complexity requires more structure.

Sales does not end at closed-won. A customer who buys but never reaches first value is weak PMF evidence. The goal is a visible **Founder-Led Sales Playbook + Hiring Gate Decision**.

## Exercise
Eric Ries warns that early hand-sold revenue can be misleading: "This approach is fundamentally non-scalable. Every sale requires handholding and personal attention from the founders themselves." That is acceptable early, but only if the founder turns the work into learning.

### 01. Choose the sales motion hypothesis.
Pick one primary motion: self-serve, founder-led outbound, founder-led inbound, sales-led B2B, product-led growth (PLG), community/content-led, or channel/partner-led. Explain why it fits the product, buyer, trust requirement, ACV, and buying process.

### 02. Define an operational ICP.
Document company criteria, user, buyer, champion, economic buyer, pain, trigger, current alternative, budget path, and disqualification rules. A useful ICP explains why the buyer should care now.

### 03. Build the first 50-100 prospect target list.
For each prospect, capture company/account, buyer and user names, contact path, ICP reason, "why now" trigger, likely current alternative, priority score, and disqualification risk. Mark the top 20 by urgency and access, not brand prestige.

### 04. Write one primary outreach sequence.
Keep the first message under 150 words. Include who you help, the acute problem, the current alternative, the concrete outcome, any proof, and one simple ask. Add a referral version and 2-3 follow-ups that add new information instead of repeating "checking in."

### 05. Run discovery as qualification.
Ask why now, what happens if nothing changes, how the customer solves it today, what the pain costs, who else is involved, how tools like this get bought, where budget comes from, what success looks like, and what could block the deal.

### 06. Use a lightweight qualification rubric.
CHAMP-lite is enough for many early B2B motions: challenge, authority, money, prioritization, and dated next step. For higher-ACV or multi-stakeholder deals, add MEDDIC-lite: metrics, economic buyer, decision process, pain, and champion.

### 07. Define CRM stages with exit criteria.
Use stages such as prospect, contacted, replied, discovery scheduled/completed, qualified, pilot/proposal/evaluation, commercial/legal/procurement, closed won, closed lost/no decision, and onboarded/activated. A discovery call without a dated next step is not an active opportunity.

### 08. Track funnel math weekly.
Review prospects added, reply rate, meeting rate, discovery-to-qualified rate, qualified-to-proposal rate, win rate, average sales cycle, ACV/pilot value, next-step scheduled rate, top objections, lost reasons, and time to first value.

### 09. Record objections and lost reasons.
Sort them into ICP, product, proof, pricing, trust, timing, stakeholder, onboarding, or implementation issues. Objections are data about the motion, not just blockers.

### 10. Confirm onboarding and activation.
Define first value milestone, onboarding owner, activation steps, required customer actions, expected time to first value, and early success signal. Closed-won customers should reach value after purchase.

### 11. Make the sales hiring gate decision.
Sam Altman writes that tech startups need at least one founder who is, or can become, "good at sales and talking to users." Do not hire a first salesperson to discover the ICP, message, pricing, objections, and close path from scratch.

## Deliverable
Produce a **Founder-Led Sales Playbook + Hiring Gate Decision** that the whole founding team and key employees can see. It should be clear enough that another capable person could attempt the motion, even if the founder still owns it.

Jyoti Bansal's phrase is useful here: "We should call it product-market-sales fit." The playbook must connect the target customer, product promise, sales motion, and post-sale activation.

Your playbook must include:

- **Motion hypothesis:** the primary sales/acquisition motion and why it fits the product, buyer, ACV, and trust requirement.
- **Operational ICP:** target account/customer, personas, pain, trigger, current alternative, buyer path, and disqualification rules.
- **Target list rules:** how prospects are found, scored, prioritized, and disqualified, plus the first 50-100 named prospects.
- **Outreach sequence:** primary message, referral version, follow-ups, segment variants, and tracking rules.

- **Discovery guide:** qualification questions, call outcomes, next-step standards, and disqualification rules.
- **Qualification rubric:** CHAMP-lite by default, MEDDIC-lite when deal complexity requires it.
- **CRM stages:** stage definitions, exit criteria, required fields, and outcome reasons.
- **Funnel metrics:** weekly dashboard and the diagnosis tied to each conversion point.

- **Objection library:** repeated objections, lost reasons, response category, and owner/action.
- **Onboarding plan:** first value milestone, activation steps, owner, expected time to value, and failure points.
- **Hiring gate:** open or closed, with evidence.

Aaron Ross's Predictable Revenue work is a useful caution: when prospecting, qualification, closing, and account management are blurred into one vague role, the team cannot see which part of the motion is working. Keep the playbook specific.

## Exit Criteria
Move forward only when this checklist is complete. Ries's warning still applies: "Revenue alone is not a sufficient goal." The company must prove a repeatable business model, not just one-off deals.

- CHAMP, MEDDIC, and economic buyer are defined before use; PMF, ICP, and ACV follow the course-wide definitions.
- The sales motion hypothesis is named and justified.
- Operational ICP includes pain, trigger, buyer/user, current alternative, budget path, and disqualification rules.
- The first 50-100 prospects have ICP reason and "why now" evidence.

- Outreach is under 150 words and tracked by segment, source, trigger, and message.
- Discovery calls end with a dated next step or clean disqualification.
- CRM stages have exit criteria.
- Funnel math is reviewed weekly.

- Objections and lost reasons are categorized into ICP, product, proof, pricing, trust, timing, stakeholder, and onboarding issues.
- Closed-won customers reach first value.
- Sales hiring gate is explicitly open or closed.
- The Founder-Led Sales Playbook is visible to the founding team and key employees.
