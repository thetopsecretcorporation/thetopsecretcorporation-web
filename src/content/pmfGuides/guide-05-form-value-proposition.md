## Field Brief
A value proposition is not a slogan.

A **value proposition** is the reason this specific customer should care, believe you, and switch. If they cannot tell why this matters, why now, why you, and why switching is worth it, the promise is not clear yet.

## Why It Matters
Polished copy cannot save a fuzzy promise. The right customer should quickly think: "This is for me. It solves a problem I already have. It beats what I do today. I believe it."

**Weak:** "AI-powered operations platform."  
**Strong:** "For ecommerce ops managers who spend Monday mornings reconciling Shopify, NetSuite, and 3PL exports, this flags late-order risk before customers complain."

## What to Do
### 1. Pull the raw evidence
Bring forward:
- ICP.
- Painful moment.
- Customer language.
- Current alternative.
- Cost of the status quo.
- Proof you have already seen.

Label each input:
- **Observed:** seen in behavior or workflow.
- **Quoted:** said by a customer.
- **Inferred:** likely, but not directly confirmed.
- **Assumed:** still a founder guess.

### 2. Write the promise
Use this:

> For [specific customer] who [painful moment], we help them [specific outcome] without [current friction], unlike [current alternative], with proof from [evidence].

Example:

> For apparel ops managers shipping 500+ orders/day who find late orders only after support tickets arrive, we flag at-risk orders each morning using Shopify, 3PL, and carrier data, without another manual spreadsheet.

### 3. Run the two brutal tests
Ask of every feature and claim:
- **So what?** What customer outcome does this create?
- **Compared to what?** What alternative are we beating?

If you cannot answer both, cut it.

### 4. Add proof
Proof can be small if it is concrete.

Example: "Three interviewed teams showed the same Monday spreadsheet. One pilot found 14 late orders before support tickets arrived."

### 5. Clarity-test it
Put the promise in front of right-fit customers. Ask:
- "What do you think this does?"
- "Who is it for?"
- "What would you compare it to?"
- "What part feels believable or not believable?"
- "Would this make you change anything this week?"

## What Counts
You are done when a right-fit customer can repeat the promise in plain language and connect it to a painful current workaround.
