## Field Brief
MVP does not mean "bad product." It means "smallest thing that creates real learning."

An **MVP** is the smallest test that teaches whether customers want the promised outcome. It may be a manual service, demo video, no-code workflow, clickable prototype, paid pilot, or narrow software slice.

## Why It Matters
Founders overbuild to avoid the scary question: does anyone care?

A good MVP tests one dangerous assumption before you spend months on dashboards, integrations, permissions, automation, edge cases, and polish.

## What to Do
### 1. Name the question
Write the one thing this test must answer:
- Will the customer urgently want this outcome?
- Will they pay or commit?
- Can we deliver the result manually?
- Does it fit the real workflow?
- Can we reach this customer?

### 2. Define the smallest complete value moment
The value moment is the smallest complete result where the customer can say, "That helped."

Examples:
- Billing audit: one recoverable overcharge found.
- Hiring tool: one qualified candidate shortlist sent to the hiring manager.
- Search product: one useful search completed.

### 3. Cut everything else
If you can do it manually for 3-5 customers, do it manually.

Do not build:
- Admin dashboards.
- Complex onboarding.
- Full integrations.
- Edge cases.
- Fancy analytics.
- Multi-person permissions.

Unless one of those is required to reach the value moment, it is probably hiding from the test.

### 4. Choose the MVP format
Pick the cheapest format that lets a real customer feel the promised result.

Examples:
- Dropbox: demo video before full file-sync infrastructure.
- Zappos-style test: post local-store shoes online, then buy and ship manually after orders.
- Claims-audit software: manually review three clinics' denied claims and send a weekly recoverable-revenue report before building the platform.

### 5. Set pass/fail criteria before launch
Define:
- Who will test it.
- What they must do.
- What value event counts.
- What commitment counts.
- What result means continue, change, or stop.

## What Counts
You are done when a right-fit customer reaches the promised outcome and your team learns whether to continue, change, or kill the idea.
