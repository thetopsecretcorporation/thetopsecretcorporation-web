## Field Brief
Before you automate the machine, make sure the result is worth automating.

**Manual delivery** means the founder does the work by hand so the customer still gets the outcome. It is not a hack. It is how you learn what the product must become.

## Why It Matters
Software hides weak value. Manual delivery exposes it.

When you serve the first customers yourself, you see where they get stuck, what they value, what they ignore, what they ask for again, and what they would pay to keep.

## What to Do
### 1. Pick 1-3 customers this week
Choose customers with:
- Acute pain.
- Direct access to the user or buyer.
- A result you can deliver by hand.
- A real commitment.
- A workflow likely to repeat across the ICP.

Do not pick impressive logos that turn you into a custom consulting shop.

### 2. Promise one result
Use this:

> We will help [customer] achieve [specific result] by [date] without [pain/friction].

Example: "We will help the billing manager find recoverable denied claims by Friday without reviewing 300 PDFs manually."

### 3. Run the manual loop
Keep it simple:
1. Recruit.
2. Onboard.
3. Collect files/access/context.
4. Deliver the result.
5. Measure value.
6. Ask for the next commitment.

### 4. Watch what should become product
Log:
- Founder time spent.
- Steps repeated every time.
- Customer confusion.
- Data or access bottlenecks.
- Result delivered.
- Customer reaction.
- Repeat request or expansion request.

Examples:
- Airbnb: founders helped New York hosts improve listings and photos; First Round reports weekly revenue doubled from about $200 to $400 after the photo experiment.
- AI hiring: manually create the first 10 candidate shortlists and see whether hiring managers use them.
- Invoice review: send a Friday PDF report from a founder-built spreadsheet before automating anything.

### 5. Decide what to automate later
Automate repeated steps that create value. Do not automate confusion, edge cases, or things customers do not care about.

## What Counts
You are done when customers receive the promised result by hand and you know what should stay manual, what should become product, and what should be cut.
