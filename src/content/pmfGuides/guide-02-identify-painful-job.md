## Field Brief
A painful job is not "they might like this." It is a real moment where something breaks badly enough that the customer already spends time, money, attention, or political capital trying to fix it.

A **job-to-be-done** means the progress a customer is trying to make in a specific situation. Use the term only if it helps you stay concrete.

**Weak:** "Sales teams need better notes."  
**Strong:** "When an account executive finishes a discovery call and has three follow-ups due before the next meeting, they need messy notes turned into accurate CRM updates so the deal does not stall."

## Why It Matters
Weak pain needs pushing. Strong pain pulls.

Good startup problems have cost: lost revenue, wasted hours, churn, compliance risk, angry customers, weekend work, executive pressure, embarrassment, or missed deadlines.

## What to Do
### 1. Write the painful-job sentence
Use this:

> When [specific trigger], [specific customer] needs to [specific outcome], because [stakes].

Examples:
- "When a property manager gets 40 maintenance texts after a storm, she needs to triage urgent repairs before tenants leave bad reviews or withhold rent."
- "When a controller closes books on the third business day, she needs to reconcile Stripe, Shopify, and bank deposits without spending Saturday in spreadsheets."

### 2. Reconstruct the last time it happened
Ask for a real episode:
- Who had the problem?
- When did it happen?
- What triggered it?
- What did they do first?
- What tools, people, or spreadsheets got involved?
- What did it cost?
- What happened because it was not solved cleanly?

If nobody can describe a recent occurrence, you do not have a real pain yet.

### 3. Separate problem from product
Do not write: "They need our AI dashboard."

Write: "They need to catch late shipments before VIP customers complain. Today they compare Shopify, 3PL exports, and support tickets every Monday morning."

### 4. Score the pain
Rate each 1-5, with evidence attached:
- Frequency.
- Intensity.
- Money/time/risk cost.
- Urgency.
- Named owner.
- Existing workaround.

## What Counts
You are done when you have one painful-job sentence and at least three real examples from the same ICP showing the pain happened recently.
